# Mach — The Winning VC Angle (with competitor study) + One-Liners

**Goal: the one-liner and framing that makes a VC see a 100x outcome. Built from what's actually getting funded in 2026, not guesses.**

---

## 1. What VCs are actually paying for right now (the evidence)

I studied the live market. Three findings decide your angle:

**Finding A — The 100x story VCs are buying is "Services-as-Software."**
The thesis sweeping a16z, Sequoia, Menlo, General Catalyst and Thrive: AI agents let software compete for **labor budgets**, not just software budgets. Software is a ~$1T market; **services + labor is ~$16T**. If agents deliver work at software margins, the addressable market is **10–20× bigger** than SaaS. Sequoia calls it "Services: the new software"; General Catalyst put $1.5B and Thrive $1B+ behind it. **This is the frame that unlocks the big multiple — sell labor spend, not a tool.**

**Finding B — The bar for the multiple: real revenue, outcomes pricing, a data moat.**
The companies trading at 50x ARR all share the same traits. Sierra: **$200M ARR, $15.8B valuation** (May 2026). Decagon: **$4.5B** at $35M ARR. What they have in common — and what VCs now demand after the 2025 hype reset:
- **Outcome-based pricing** (charge for value/work delivered, not seats).
- **Production-grade reliability** (99%+, "it actually does the job").
- **A moat from proprietary data + sticky integrations** that make switching costly.
- **Real revenue + retention** — "moved beyond concept risk."
- Customer service is the **#1 funded agent category (~42% of agent capital)** — a proven wedge.

**Finding C — The cautionary tale that makes Mach's structure look smart.**
Crypto AI-agent tokens (Virtuals, ai16z) **collapsed — Virtuals' revenue fell 96%** and its token is down ~87%, because the agents made money but **the token captured none of it** — speculation, not real revenue or value capture. The lesson every crypto-adjacent founder must answer: *"where does real revenue come from, and how does value actually accrue to holders?"* Mach's answer (real customer payments + on-chain ownership of a compounding asset) is the **post-collapse, grown-up version** of that trade.

## 2. The single most winning angle for Mach

Combine all three findings into one position:

> **Mach turns AI agents into income-producing assets you can own — attacking the $16T labor market with software margins, and capturing the value on-chain that the last generation of agent tokens threw away.**

Why this is the 100x frame, in plain terms:

1. **It's a labor-spend story, not a tool story** → the 10–20× TAM that justifies a venture-scale outcome (Finding A).
2. **The agents earn real revenue from real customers today** (SMB sales, content campaigns) → clears the post-hype "show me outcomes" bar (Finding B).
3. **The moat is the strongest kind VCs underwrite — proprietary, compounding data** — but Mach goes one better: that moat is an **ownable, tradeable, on-chain asset that gets more valuable the longer it runs.** Switching cost is total; the agent can't be cloned (Finding B).
4. **It fixes the exact thing that killed Virtuals** — value capture. Revenue is real and accrues to owners on-chain (Finding C).
5. **Unfair distribution + unfair market** — existing SEA SMB distribution (Senangchat) and a halal/SEA market **no Western VC-backed competitor can reach** ($4T Islamic finance, 2B users). VCs love a wedge incumbents structurally can't follow.

**The positioning in one breath:** *Sierra and Decagon sell you one AI employee. Mach lets anyone launch, own, and invest in an entire workforce of them — and turns AI labor into an asset class.*
